(Engine, decision) Skinned-mesh culling runs CONSERVATIVE by default: the
cone test and the HZB occlusion test are SKIPPED for skinned draws/clusters
(sphere + frustum + contribution only; the deformed spheres/unions are
SkinDump cross-checked sound). Evidence trail: the fit itself is verified
honest (TOURMALINE_SKINBOUNDS_DUMP's CPU-reference cross-check agreed
2000/2000, zero field mismatches) and ~42% of the knight's posed clusters
degenerate their cones anyway (the spread is real geometry) — but the fog
evidence (a complete depth silhouette while Main misses meshlets) pinned
the visible-chunk drops on the lossy filter stages, so skinned geometry
stays off both until the residual dropper is isolated. This mirrors
Remedy's own shipped call: Alan Wake 2 rebuilt cones (~0.1 ms) but SHIPPED
with meshlet cone culling disabled — their HZB occlusion covered the win.
TOURMALINE_SKIN_CONE=1 restores the skinned cone test (A/B lever); the
rebuild + the cone-axis overlay always run (honest data either way). The
HZB skip for skinned has no lever yet — add one if the occluder leg is
ever exonerated. The SkinDump diagnostic stays until the investigation
fully closes (remove in the squash).

(Engine, resolved-verdict) Skinned-mesh self-shadowing on thin double-skin
meshes (the knight's shield): the shadow chain is the standard robust one
(receiver-side normal bias ~1.25 texels world + slope-scaled oriented depth
bias ~0.5 texels/depthRange, per-cascade texel-scaled, UI scales in the
Debug panel) and it detaches self-overlap up to ~a few mm. The residual is
geometric, not a bias bug: shadow depth renders with Cull Front (back-face
depth — the classic closed-mesh anti-acne choice), so on a THIN OPEN shell
(~2mm skins) the stored back skin sits 2mm behind the receiver skin instead
of a closed mesh's thickness, and at grazing sun angles the required
detachment grows unbounded while the baked bias is fixed (any bias big
enough to clear it would peter-pan real contact shadows globally). RIDE
WITH IT. Content-side fixes: close the shell rim (a solid edge strip makes
it a closed volume — the back-face trick then works as designed) or widen
the skin gap beyond the bias budget (~>1 cm). Engine-side answer for thin
shells if it ever matters: EVSM (variance maps leak light through tiny
overlaps naturally — the toggle exists, default PCF).
